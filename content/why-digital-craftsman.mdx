---
title: "Why digital craftsman"
publishedAt: "2025-03-12"
---

I am not a software developer.

Well, if we take the term literally, I suppose that could be true — I do develop software after all. However, I never considered titles such as *programmer* or *software engineer* a good fit for me; they always seem to emphasize the coding aspect, which isn't my sole focus.

So when I first discovered the term digital craftsman through [Takuya Matsuyama](https://www.craftz.dog/) (whom I encourage you to follow), something finally clicked: I realized that what I'm truly passionate about is **building**, to pursue an idea and expand upon it.

This often goes beyond coding itself, chasing an idea that intrigues you can easily lead to the foreign lands of design, writing, engineering, or even beyond that, stretching through the realms of art and music.

By letting curiosity guide you into the unknown, it opens the door for creating something far more meaningful — a kind of refined work that distinguishes itself from the rest, that makes all of the passion that has been put into it self evident.

The term digital craftsman carries this meaning with it. 

When I think of a craftsman, I picture an elderly man working in his workshop, perhaps making [traditional Chinese teapots](https://youtu.be/PheDccVeOaE), fine-tuning the smallest details that will remain unseen by most.

This art of polishing something to its limit is something that greatly inspires me. 

Even if no one cares about that small detail, I still do. Even if no one can feel the rough edges, I still do. So I won't publish something unless I've polished it to the limit of my capacity.

It's not just for my own satisfaction, or for that one person who will notice the detail, but also out of respect for the craft.

That's why I began calling myself a digital craftsman.
